Heritage

Pakistan sits on the Indus river and on old trade routes. Cities, monasteries and forts from several civilisations survive, and several are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Sindh
Mohenjo-daro, Makli Necropolis
Punjab
Taxila, Rohtas Fort, Lahore Fort
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
Takht-i-Bahi
Tip
Visit in cooler months and bring water and a hat

Ancient cities

Mohenjo-daro in Sindh was a planned Indus Valley city about 4,500 years ago, with brick streets and drains. Taxila, near Islamabad, was a centre of Gandhara learning and has stupas and monasteries.

Takht-i-Bahi near Mardan is a Buddhist monastery on a hilltop, still standing with its courtyards and cells.

Forts and tombs

Rohtas Fort near Jhelum was begun in 1541 under Sher Shah Suri and has huge gates. The Makli Necropolis near Thatta is one of the largest burial grounds in the world, with tombs from several dynasties.

Lahore Fort and Shalimar Gardens are covered on the Lahore page.
